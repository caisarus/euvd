# Speaking venues — the live picture

> Checked 2026-08-23, after the owner declined DefCamp. Every CfP status below was read
> from the organiser's own page that day.

## First, the thing worth saying plainly

**Nothing in this project depends on a talk.** Speaking is a Phase 4 *visibility* item.
The NLnet application does not ask for one, the launch does not need one, and no release
is gated on one. If you never stand on a stage for euvd-watch, the plan still completes.

The reason it was on the list at all is that a talk is a compact way to reach practitioners
and to have something citable. There are cheaper ways to get both, below.

## The two that are still worth an email

### Nordic Software Security Summit — closed 20 August, but ask anyway

4–6 November, Stockholm. Its topic list reads like this project's table of contents:
regulatory compliance, software supply chain security, SBOMs, vulnerability management,
and **EU regulations including the Cyber Resilience Act**.

Submission is an **email to a named person** (`info@nsss.se`), not a portal that hard-closes
— their own page says "send proposals, even if you don't have all the details". A small
conference three days past its deadline will often still read a well-matched proposal.
Travel budget is limited and sponsor-dependent, so say up front if you need it.

**Cost of asking: one email.** Say the word and I will write it.

### FOSDEM 2027 — the real target, if you want a stage

Early February, Brussels. Free, no registration, and the audience is exactly right: the
security devroom, and possibly the distributions or software-supply-chain devrooms if they
run. Calls typically open in autumn. **Nothing is published for 2027 yet**, so this is a
watch item, not a deadline.

The DefCamp abstract converts to a FOSDEM submission in an afternoon — FOSDEM slots are
shorter, so it becomes the middle three sections plus the confession.

## Better value than a conference, for what this project actually needs

Ranked by what they do for the CodeSupply application and for adoption, against effort:

1. **A slot on an OpenSSF working-group call.** Already step 12 of the action plan. Ten to
   fifteen minutes as an agenda item on a public video call. No CfP, no travel, no stage,
   no deadline — you email the WG chair and ask. It reaches the people who decide whether
   other scanners consume the EUVD mapping, which is the entire thesis of the funding
   application. **This is strictly more useful than DefCamp would have been.**
2. **The written technical post.** Already drafted, already live: the EUVD API write-up at
   <https://caisarus.github.io/euvd/docs/euvd-api/> is the r/netsec submission for launch
   week. A good technical post and a conference talk do the same job; the post is done, is
   linkable forever, and does not require standing up.
3. **Podcasts.** No CfP, no calendar, one hour of your time. The open-source and appsec
   shows take guests by email. Lower stakes than a stage and often a wider audience.
4. **Attend FOSDEM without speaking.** Free, no registration. The hallway conversations
   with CycloneDX and OpenSSF people are the part that would help the project; the stage
   is optional.

## What happens to the drafted abstract

Nothing is wasted. `plans/talks/defcamp-2026-abstract.md` is venue-independent — the title,
the seven-part outline, the takeaways and the framing around the false negative all stand.
It converts to NSSS, to FOSDEM, or to a long-form article without being rewritten.

The bio in `plans/talks/defcamp-2026-bio.md` is also reusable as-is: a Sessionize speaker
profile is filled in once and reused for every conference you ever submit to, so if you
ever do submit anywhere, that work is done.
